Pretraining

There's only one task behind all of it: predict the next token. Show the model trillions of tokens, hide the next one, let it guess, and nudge its weights toward the right answer — billions of times. Press play and watch a model teach itself to write.

A recorded run, sped up. A real pretraining run of a model like this takes weeks on thousands of GPUs and burns through ~15 trillion tokens. The loss values and sample stages here are authored to show the shape of what happens; the mechanism — predict, measure surprise, adjust — is exactly real.

B

The loop that does it

Every step is the same four moves. There's no labeling, no human in the loop — the text itself is both the question and the answer, which is why the internet can be the training set. This is self-supervised learning.

The result is a base model: a brilliant autocomplete that has read a huge slice of humanity's writing. It is not yet an assistant — ask it a question and it may just continue with more questions. Teaching it to answer is Stage 4.

Why small open models keep punching above their weight. The compute-optimal recipe (Chinchilla) says about 20 tokens per parameter. Modern open models blow past that — 27B on 15T tokens is ~550× the parameters in data — deliberately. Over-training a small model costs more up front but produces something far cheaper to run forever. That's why a 27B model you can fit on a laptop keeps out-punching its size.
